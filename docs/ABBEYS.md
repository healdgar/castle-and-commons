# Abbey institutions and distinct order policies

The first adapter is Cistercian, available through `institutionalCapability` from 1128. This follows the accepted rules in `SYSTEMS.md` decisions 1–2 and 12: granted marginal ground, self-worked granges, actual lay brothers/sisters and hired households, wool, fish, alms and hospitality. Benedictine houses are available from 850 and Augustinian canons from 1100, through the same technology eligibility and genuine funded founding path. Those orders accept unheld cultivated demesne and may receive the landlord title of an existing lease; its actual tenant retains the work right and customary crop share. Early Cistercians retain their marginal-land and no-rent rules. From 1349 a real paid order review may license letting an existing cultivated grange when the actual landlord receipt exceeds the crop that available self-work can earn; the native tenant must retain enough crop to support its household. The 850 and 1066 starts do not create anachronistic Cistercians.

## Canonical records and conservation

An abbey is one `corpFound('abbey', ...)` account in `W.abbeys`, identified by `abbey:N`. Founding transfers silver from the real founder and grants actual unheld demesne titles in free alms. Complete native raster cells, including their intersected Voronoi parcels, must belong to the grant; a guild, household, tenant or unknown explicit holder cannot be silently displaced. The political settlement owner is unchanged. Canonical `accountOwner` and `propertyRightsResolve` resolve institution keys and `household:hhN`; household beneficiary/head identity remains numeric for inheritance.

`abbeyFounder` resolves Crown grants to the actual `W.monarch`; house zero has no separate head. Other founders remain their great house's named living head. Founding retains its actual piety, adopted order, positive closed monthly surplus, treasury reserve and lawful title gates. Manorial lease endowments likewise require the actual living, qualified founder, positive surplus, permitted order policy and its lawful landlord title. A missing, dead or unpious ruler cannot substitute another house's qualified head. The annual foregone crop is compared with twelve times the actual monthly surplus; no year's waiting period is required.

Members are actual named people retained on the native population roll. Admission needs an actual roof, one year's food, a personal entry gift and no existing assigned land or work. A dependent splits from the existing household; the parents' estate is retained. Personal movable goods transfer through native ownership. Death, hunger and age apply to the same people. Professed members do not marry or reproduce. No institutional `_hallN`, abstract religious cohort, opening cash, food or generated people is added.

After profession, a movable legacy to a member passes to the actual abbey account. Native land succession excludes professed members as personal holders; it retains the other heirs' actual customary rights. The parent integration additionally requires actual villein release before admission and excludes active institutional jobs.

Daily wages go to real native household heads. Hired servants retain their families, mortality and household pantries; their exclusive institutional work replaces ordinary producer/trade activity. Hiring uses the real household's bread cost and past earnings. Insufficient funding, siege, absence or relocation releases only that institution's job markers. Actual teaching and other held jobs are preserved. A cellarer is an actual available living member, with `officeFor:'abbey:N'`; daily selection cannot replace an employed master's office or leave two cellarers.

## Daily dependencies

- `tickAbbeyWork`, after `peoplePrepare`, pays today's actual servants and derives cultivated grant area and work from living, fed lay members and paid households. Native `tickEconomy` excludes this area from generic crop output, then credits the institution's actual cultivated output once. Unworked granted fields do not produce a corporate crop. Tithe and multure follow the real charter and mill; early Cistercian grants carry no serf rent or appropriated parish tithe.
- `tickAbbeyMeals`, after `tickMarket` and before `tickPopulation`, purchases/transfers actual grain and fish into the same native member/corrody pantries. Native fallback buying and eating honor the four-footed-meat prohibition. Native `eatHouseholds` consumes each mouth once. Boarding a returning local traveller uses that person's actual household pantry, not a second guest population.
- `tickAbbeys`, after `tickUpkeep`, checks founding, admission, clearing, charity, corrodies and further compound commissions from today's actual balances. New paid construction starts at tomorrow's projects phase. Late admission refreshes the native household roll and does not supply a second meal.

Sheep and hay investment eligibility is daily. Purchases use current native sellers, actual self-worked grazing, spare fed living lay/paid work, expected yield and the institution's bread reserve; repeated days cannot allocate the same beasts or money twice. September 14 retains its actual annual General Chapter cadence. A named leader travels on the native errand path to a port for the General Chapter; the home and order keep separate purses, with actual board/fare payments and explicit flows abroad. No guessed order income or automatic percentage tithe funds the journey.

Actual older Cistercian houses form an acyclic mother/daughter relationship. The mother sends its actual living leader on a native annual visitation when the daughter can fund the trip and the mother can support the visitor. Payment occurs at arrival, from daughter to mother. The visitor reads actual roofs, board, member hunger, overdue debts and charter breaches. Uncorrected findings restrict optional expansion; a repeated finding with another real qualified member can require election of a different named leader. A successful paid General Chapter journey also records the order's review on return. These proceedings grant no new people or goods.

Later grange leasing requires that recorded paid review, the later historical policy and actual labour scarcity. Its comparison uses adopted crop yield, parish tithe, mill multure and the real tenant/landlord shares. It preserves the abbey's native title and gives the work right to one existing viable household; it does not receive a new village, serfs or an appropriated tithe. Native hunting parks and warrens are excluded from automatic alms grants, clearing and compound footprints.

The wider Church continuation runs `churchWork` inside early `tickAbbeyWork`, so existing clergy receive today's real wages before production and family decisions. `tickChurchBodies`, inside late `tickChurches`, handles closed-receipt assessments, actual remittances, appointments, ecclesiastical records, and paid church fabric. ## Native compound and water operations

Church, residence, grange and guest house/almonry use the shared paid compound project engine and exact fixed native building/access geometry. Required materials are real owner stock or native purchases, and work advances only with actual wages. Abbey churches are distinct from the parish church and do not claim a parish churchyard. Daily vacancy letting preserves the corporate title and selects an actual household for a lease only when its expected retained crop supports that household. Generic crop production includes leased fields, with native ownership splitting the real crop between tenant and abbey. Only fields whose native owner and worker are both the abbey enter the self-worked crop pass; granted tenant fields never become an invented lay workforce.

Canonical buildings supply storage and housing; the compound owns neither a second capacity nor another inventory.

A fishpond is a paid contained river reservoir in canonical `s.lake`, only where the settlement has no existing reservoir. The entire retained pond/dam footprint must be granted, terrain must close the water, and no native road/building may be flooded. Completion consumes an actual purchased fish stocking input. Output waits a full growing year and requires today's actual paid fisher; drought and winter affect it. Fish remains the existing native commodity. Initial throughput is an explicit game calibration, not a claim about historical load weights. The pond reuses native masks, route fingerprints, minimap, water surface and dam drawing, with a dedicated worker-to-render hydraulic dirty flag. Its component references the same lake and carries no store or capacity.

Sheep are actual purchased beasts in the abbey's native owner ledger, subject to native breeding, mortality and winter hay. Milking/shearing need real spare lay or paid work. Native market/cargo sales credit actual buyers' silver; wool quantities and receipts are recorded at `lordSold`. Forward-sale contracts use the separate native credit adapter's `forwardOffer`, when present, on actual newly produced output.

Charity gives only actual grain above the institution's annual food reserve. A sound hospital and today's actual paid, present hospitaller can serve visiting native errand travellers during their actual stay. The existing homeward leg records `visitSince`, the outward arrival, while the same person remains on the home roll. Hospitality consumes actual held grain or fish above the annual reserve and records `p.boardReceipt={day,si,meal,source}`. The receipt is food already eaten, not inventory; the native home household buys and consumes only its remaining meal. Actual scholar and other hosted meals share a maximum of one meal per person. Outbound and homeward transit receive no invented hospitality. Corrodies transfer a named elderly household's real up-front consideration for board for life; they create no population. The school component has a native paid classroom plan and completion reference `a.school={building,ownerId}`. The schooling system owns teacher employment, pupil enrolment, tuition, stipends and board and may add `schoolId`; no pupils or learning are inferred from the building label.

`tools/institution-lifecycle.test.mjs` checks native errands, actual ledger consumption, household meal and cash conservation, partial scholar board, reserves and institution-specific job release. Generated-world founding and cold continuation remain serialized coordinator gates.

Benedictine and Augustinian houses may appropriate a parish only when the real patron holds its native title, an actual funded bishop assents, and the abbey can hire an actual available vicar. Native parish funds remain separate. The vicar's portion stays with the parish; the abbey receives only actual newly produced great-tithe grain. Loss of today's vicar service suspends the abbey's share. Cistercians cannot use this adapter. Abbey church upgrades commission native paid fabric projects; they do not alter the building instantly.

The nunnery flag is sex, not a separate economic order: the available Cistercian/Benedictine/Augustinian rule can be founded for male or female native people. Journaled foundation controls offer both sexes for each available rule and verify the real settlement owner. Female admission requires actual eligible women; the named leader is an abbess and lay members are lay sisters. Inspecting the choices does not create a person or spend the endowment.

Friaries are distinct mendicant communities. `SYSTEMS.md` mentions their historical arrival and existing drawn foundations, but its #35 actor/proposal list and accepted decision 1 specify endowed monastic houses and the Cistercian/Benedictine adapters. A native `friary` building therefore grants no implied friars, wage, preaching, income or abstract religious population through this adapter. Operative mendicant recruitment/board/preaching would need its own explicit institution and is not claimed as completed here.

## Own-use grinding and leather

Accepted decision 1 now has actual paid own-use mill and tannery clients. Both retain the shared compound's fixed native building and reachable approach, check the entire granted ground union, consume real materials and pay native construction workers. A private mill keeps its corporate title and leaves the settlement's `s.mill`, manorial mill farm and public grinding operator selection intact. Private own-use mill/tannery rooms are unavailable to ordinary master leases and apprenticeships; actual prior craft qualification still supports native abbey operator recruitment. A private mill also cannot veto a separate native public-windmill proposal. Water mills require the existing fitted river bank, current wet wheel position and a genuine retained intake/feed where present; windmills require locally adopted wind power and actual exposure. Additional water wheels and races use their native geometry in the drawing and its invalidation key.

A qualified conversus can retain an actual pre-profession craft; otherwise a real qualified, available household head receives an actual wage. Assigned millers and tanners cannot also supply a second field, flock, ordinary craft or construction workday. Grinding uses the same finite `b.poweredDay` as public grinding and other powered services. Only genuine public work creates multure claims and the existing operator/title-holder receipts; the abbey's already-supported own operator collects no second multure. A mill investment subtracts a proposed conversus operator from the retained field hands, then compares real grange throughput and saved service against capital and available operator costs. A continuing paid miller needs a whole annual wage; a small grange may legitimately refuse that investment. Insufficient power, title, adoption, qualified staff or surplus remains a genuine refusal.

The small own-use tannery is a paid 4 × 4 m shed and pits, with four timber loads and thirty construction days. Native cattle slaughter supplies one hide load per beast and sheep slaughter 0.15; these are explicit physical game units. The actual slaughterer owns the skins once. Transfers, livestock sales, births, disease deaths, pigs and hunting create no skins. `hides` and `leather` start at zero, have zero ordinary household demand, and use the native canonical held/sale balances, facilities, spoilage and purchases.

One hide load consumes 0.25 timber loads as a bark-material equivalent and yields 0.75 usable leather loads. Timber is debited from the same real stock used by building, not from another bark inventory. Actual covered native member, corrody and paid servant household mouths wear 0.10 leather loads per year; aliases of one household count once. These units are initial physical assumptions, not historical weights or fixed market prices. Real footwear consumption is bounded by available leather. At a shortage below two days' wear, the house books only enough finite work for up to thirty days' cover; existing stock releases the tanner before next day's ordinary work. A hired tanner is paid for the actual booked day, while idle days earn no institutional wage. The provision quote protects the real food reserve and funds actual inputs, materials and expected batch service; it does not impose a commercial profit requirement on a house's own footwear.

Inside the unchanged daily order, early `tickAbbeyWork` assigns or releases the exclusive operator before deriving cultivated land and flock hands. Native `tickLivestock` slaughter can supply today's production; butcher slaughter later in `tickMarket` supplies future days. `tickEconomy` processes actual crop grinding and then the institution's finite leather recipe and wear. Paid compound completion follows production, and closing commissions first work tomorrow. No render read decides work or pays anyone.

`millGround`, `hidesTanned`, `barkUsed`, `leatherMade` and `leatherUsed` describe actual current-year work, `prev` retains the closed year, and `industryTotal` retains cumulative actual work. These counters are observations, not inventory or capacity. Detached summaries and `abbeyNativeStateExpression` expose them, actual operator identities/paid days and `industryPlans` refusal reasons. The existing native founding/completion criteria remain unchanged. ## Funded skilled household journeys

Local actual qualified operators take precedence. A funded Abbey service can invite a qualified native household from one of the eight nearest settlements. The shortlist comes from the canonical craft indexes and is rebuilt when those indexes change; current freedom, household membership, jobs, land/work rights, route, food, title and money remain live checks. Each outside settlement contributes at most twelve available qualified heads. This avoids a new realm-wide people scan for each ordinary tanning batch.

A service offer must improve the actual household's expected livelihood after ordinary room rent, food, kinship and relocation costs. A tanner's offer values only genuine expected batch days, rather than an imaginary annual full-time salary. A singleton may instead choose an actually eligible professed place with the real entry gift, adopted rule, food reserve and completed residential capacity. An external mill offer requires an existing retained powered component or paid project. A proposed tannery additionally needs genuine wear, available inputs and a lawful funded worksite, including actual corridor compensation when due.

The Abbey transfers real travelling expenses into the existing household account, then moves those same people through native `journey`, `departHousehold` and `arriveHousehold`. Household assets and craft qualifications travel with them. Transit consumes actual bought or held food and records the same one-meal receipt used by other guests. Neither salary nor profession begins remotely. At actual arrival, ordinary native household placement must supply a payable room; a professed recruit must still pass the actual Abbey roof, food and personal-gift checks. A lost roof, failed funding, siege, dead recruit or unavailable service returns the original living household on a second native journey. Accepted alternative work closes the offer without removing that job.

Early `tickAbbeyWork` checks actual arrival/return before today's staffing, work and meals; closing `tickAbbeys` proposes new funded journeys after current wages, output and upkeep. Plain bounded `skilledOffers` records retain the actual actor/account references and journey dates. Detached summaries and the native fixture expose scalar identity, status, destination, quoted service days/capital, actual travelling payments and refusal reasons. The card labels these observations **Skilled recruitment** and **Travelling recruits**. No new decision phase or render-owned policy is introduced.
