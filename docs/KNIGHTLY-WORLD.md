# Knightly service and institutions (#41)

Native implementation, awaiting the coordinator's integrated gate. This intentionally changes medieval history; one-day, worker, fast-forward, save and replay must still execute the same operations in the same order.

A great house is the same account through succession. The existing first living heir now inherits even when a minor. A dated relief is assessed from the house's observed annual receipts at the customary one-twelfth rate; unresolved earlier relief remains due on the same house. The realm's existing legal majority remains sixteen. Wardship takes only realised positive estate surplus after actual monthly military expenses, preserving the same operating chest as ordinary household spending. Death, majority, expiry and extinct ward buyers end or revert the actual right. A named buyer bids only when its actual steward advantage, less maintenance, exceeds the Crown's expected remaining income, and pays from its actual chest.

A royal war saves each annual call. A house sends its existing host for forty days or pays actual scutage from annual receipts. The cost comparison uses the host's actual rate and local food price. No house can do both for the same call. Once parliament exists, the existing real attendance, wages, travel and voting mechanism decides commutation consent; refusal prevents collection and does not generate a movable-wealth grant. Active service explicitly makes that house a royal ally in native military hostility; expiry ends that alliance and recalls the actual host. Private feuds remain separate.

A tournament requires at least two actual living, skilled notable riders with actual titled horses. Real local carpenters build the lists through paid daily work and purchased, consumed timber/tools. The prize is transferred into an actual escrow chest before proclamation. The riders travel as the same native notable or notable/head alias, with actual arrival, board and return. Their current-day actual food receipt fills only that same person's remaining need and reduces its native home household and own-seat hall meal; the seat's proxy retinue stays home. Bouts use riding/arms and the institutions RNG stream. The loser pays an actual horse ransom or conveys one actual horse title. The escrow pays the winner, or refunds the real host on cancellation/no attendance. Unperformed work and unavailable prizes create no receipts, horses, or actor copies. The former 700-coin petition stand-in commissions this same operation.

Paid men may remain together after a war. A company retains the exact discharged actors and finite horses, with a named native captain and initially empty company purse. Actual members can contribute bread money beyond their families' subsistence. Existing foreign sell-swords who stay become the same resident actors with actual household accounts; no second population is generated. Hiring needs a named employer at the company's actual town, a funded customary month's wage, and local food costs. Native houses/Crown skip ordinary host wages for companies, whose exact contract wages flow employer → company → soldiers' actual households. Unemployed hungry men join the native outlaw system as the same people; dissolution returns actual people, finite horses and remaining money once.

A chivalric order after 1348 requires a real chapel, actual lettered/scriptural celibate head and actual founder funds. Paid chapel services appoint the real chaplain; an empty title creates neither priest nor income. Membership is recorded only for actual adult riders at the chapel whose houses pay the dues. Annual accounts pay actual services and record actual members' military service, with a surviving order chest included in cash reports.

Actual heiresses and widowed house heads seek leave from the Crown or their real wardship buyer before a native marriage. The customary fine is one month of the same house's observed receipts. A paid writ goes to an existing available lettered clerk; it does not manufacture a clerk, principal or land dispute. The prudent policy waives leave when the widow lacks spare money, the fine cannot pay the writ, or no actual clerk is available. Charge, waive and refuse policies and individual pardons are journaled. Repeat attempts with the same partner cannot charge twice, and actual religious vows, existing marriages and absence block the ceremony. Alliance negotiations are eligible daily: their daily probability preserves the former 35% survival hazard over thirty-seven days, rather than restricting decisions to every thirty-seventh day.

The first actual royal son's dubbing requires adult arms/riding skills, the household at the capital, a completed chapel, existing available clergy and smith, a titled royal horse, actual tools/cloth and a funded service bill beyond the royal reserve. It consumes the materials and pays the named service households once, retaining the same people, skills and horse title. Missing clergy leave a funded service vacancy; they never generate a priest. A first son's actual dubbing or first daughter's actual native wedding calls one customary aid per reign and event. The default custom assesses one twelfth of observed annual vassal-house receipts. Houses without real positive receipts or holdings owe nothing. Actual parliamentary attendance and voting determine consent once available; refusal prevents collection and creates no movable-wealth grant. Vassals weigh the cost of attending against their actual assessed liability, even when the Crown already has money. Monthly account day twenty is the real payment date; collections preserve operating reserves, retain unpaid balances, survive a head's succession and discharge extinct accounts. Zero assessments require no sitting. Completed aid records are bounded.

Public and mint recruitment preserves compatible existing office-holders but excludes actual teachers, keepers, abbey staff, pupils, apprentices, other employees and absent soldiers. Vacancy quotes retain the real employer and require that its current purse can pay the quoted work; actual appointments still require performed paid service.
